# Scientific Writing

## When to use
For proposals, abstracts, manuscripts, presentations, methods, results, discussion and responses to reviewers.

## Procedure
1. Establish audience, venue, research question, contribution and manuscript stage.
2. Read canonical model specification, verified literature matrix, experiment protocol and analysis report. Separate evidence from conjecture.
3. Structure argument: problem; established literature; precise gap; mechanism; hypotheses; model and identification; results; sensitivity; limitations; contribution.
4. For Methods, include exact initialization, four functions and parameters, firm behavior, randomness, seeds, treatments, horizons and reproducibility references.
5. For Results, report estimands, units, uncertainty, effect sizes, threshold definitions and figures with verifiable source data.
6. In Discussion, distinguish simulation-conditional insights from empirical claims; identify alternative explanations and what remains untested.
7. Verify every reference against source metadata; never invent DOI, publication, peer review, page or findings. Follow copyright quotation limits.
8. Conduct internal consistency and research-audit review before declaring a draft submission-ready.

## Output
Versioned manuscript draft, claim–evidence ledger, references and explicit open questions.

## Acceptance
Every factual or numerical claim has traceable support; research limitations are candid; no claim of publication readiness without review.
