# Experimental Design

## Research Objective

Evaluate when knowledge sharing among competing firms
creates economic value under technological heterogeneity.

## Baseline Validation

- 20 firms
- 4 production technologies
- 5 firms per technology
- 50 decision rounds
- Initial budget: 100 per firm
- Initial capital share: 0.50
- Independent random experimentation

## Learning-Curve Evaluation

Evaluate outcomes at rounds:

- 5: Early learning
- 10: Short-run learning
- 20: Medium-run learning
- 50: Long-run outcome

## Treatments

### Game 1: Independent Learning

Firms observe only their own experimental histories.

### Game 2: Competitive Learning

Firms additionally observe publicly available information
about competitors' performance.

### Game 3: Cooperative Learning

Firms may voluntarily exchange detailed experimental
histories, including unsuccessful experiments, at a cost.

## Experimental Controls

Comparisons should hold constant:

- Initial firm characteristics
- Production technologies
- Resource budgets
- Number of experimental opportunities
- Baseline random conditions
- Market environment

## Outcome Measures

### Learning

- Output efficiency
- Time to efficiency thresholds
- Experimental acceptance rate
- Allocation distance from optimal

### Economic Outcomes (Future Extensions)

- Firm profits
- Industry profits
- Consumer surplus
- Total welfare
- AI investment
- Knowledge-sharing costs

## Identification Strategy

Use matched simulation branches and repeated random seeds.

Treatment differences must arise from information access
and associated decisions, not unequal experimentation budgets.

## Current Limitations

- Firms maximize output rather than profit.
- AI investment is not yet implemented.
- Firms do not compete in a product market.
- Experimentation is costless.
- Production outcomes are deterministic.
- Knowledge transferability is not yet identified.
