# Game 2 — Competition and learning from rivals

**Status:** Research discussion / design not yet approved (2026-11-01).

## Originating research idea

Firms do not learn only from their own trial-and-error. They may observe competitors and consider learning from them. Motivations articulated by the principal researcher include fear of falling behind as rivals develop future advantages, reducing the cost of independent research and development, and discovering gaps or opportunities in the market.

Game 2 is intended to introduce competitive and observational interactions beyond the independent learning environment of Game 1.

## Inherited baseline (not a new assumption)

Game 1 has 100 heterogeneous firms, four production families (Cobb–Douglas, Leontief, Linear, CES), capital/labor allocation under a fixed budget, and independent learning policies. Its completed numerical findings and limitations are documented in `docs/games/game1/heterogeneous_findings.md`. Game 1 does not itself contain market competition, competitor imitation, strategic information disclosure, or profit maximization.

## Decisions reserved for the principal researcher

1. **What can firms observe?** Examples for discussion only: rival output, actions/inputs, performance changes, or aggregate market signals. These are alternatives, not implemented rules.
2. **What can be learned?** Examples: a rival's capital–labor allocation, a search direction, or a technological practice. Distinguish observation from knowledge transfer.
3. **When does a firm seek to learn?** Examples: relative underperformance, perceived future competitive threat, or expected R&D savings. No decision rule has been approved.
4. **What is the competitive environment?** Decide whether market demand, price, market share and profits enter this game and how they are determined.
5. **What is the benchmark?** Identify what is held fixed from Game 1 so any new outcomes can be interpreted appropriately.

## Research protocol

Discuss and approve one economic decision at a time. Only after approval, formalize the state variables, information set, choice rule, payoffs, experiment and falsifiable outcomes. Do not treat conceptual examples above as accepted assumptions. Do not claim a research gap or infer causality from the baseline simulations.
