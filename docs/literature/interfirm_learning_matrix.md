# Literature matrix — interfirm learning, competition, and knowledge sharing

**Status:** Initial verified-source screening, 2026-11-01. **Not yet a systematic literature review.** This file is the canonical living matrix; update in place, do not create version-suffixed copies. Full-text methodological extraction remains pending for many entries. Dates refer to publication year (online-first differences noted). No paper listed below directly validates Game 1's simulation coefficients or its numerical outcomes.

## Scope and screening rules

Core question: How do heterogeneous firms learn through independent search, observing rivals, and voluntary knowledge exchange, and how do competition, AI and network/partner choice change incentives and distributional outcomes?

Coverage: (A) foundational learning/search; (B) organizational search, imitation and ABM; (C) interfirm knowledge exchange/absorptive capacity; (D) technology/AI diffusion and competitive spillovers; (E) partner choice, incentives and knowledge protection. Prioritize 2020–2026 but retain indispensable foundations.

**Evidence labels:** `Direct-topic` means the paper studies a closely matching substantive mechanism, *not* that it directly verifies our model. `Conceptual` means theoretical framing only. `Adjacent` means an empirical/methodological parallel requiring care. Abstract-level screening must not be mistaken for full-text replication.

## First-pass synthesis (hypotheses, NOT established gaps)

1. **Game 1:** Comparing Guided and Random under four production families and heterogeneous parameters is a simulation design. Existing search literature means novelty cannot be claimed from the mere existence of adaptive search or heterogeneous agents. Check whether a production-function-geometry explanation is genuinely distinct from existing NK and local-search studies.
2. **Game 2:** Guida et al. (2024), Bilgin et al. (2025) and Liu et al. (2026) imply that exploration, imitation and rivalry are already well studied separately or jointly. The potential contribution may lie in **endogenous willingness to learn from rivals under competition and AI-driven productivity change**, if not already covered in prior models.
3. **Game 3:** Wu et al. (2023), Fan et al. (2026), Friedmann et al. (2025), and Bouncken & Vogt (2026) directly challenge a generic claim that voluntary interfirm sharing and knowledge networks are novel. Investigate whether **partner choice × transfer effectiveness × strategic protection × cost/payoff allocation × technological heterogeneity** offers an identifiable, nontrivial incremental mechanism.
4. **AI:** D'Amico et al. (2025/2026) cautions against presuming that AI universally complements external knowledge collaboration; empirical evidence points to potential substitution for some external partners.
5. **Validation:** Distinguish code/unit testing, Monte Carlo replication, mechanism ablation, parameter sensitivity, empirical calibration and external validity. Current Game 1 results establish only a subset.
